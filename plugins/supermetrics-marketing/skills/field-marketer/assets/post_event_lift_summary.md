# Post-event lift summary template

The structured doc reporting on whether a physical event drove measurable digital engagement lift in the target region. Audience is usually a mix: the field marketing manager who ran the event (wants to know what to repeat or change next time), and the marketing leader (wants to know if the event was worth the spend). Write for both.

The constraint that shapes everything: post-event measurement is messy. Lift can come from the event itself, from supporting paid promotion, from general regional trends, or from coincidence. The honest version of this doc names what's confounded.

---

# [Event name] post-event measurement
**Event date:** [Date]
**Region:** [City / metro / state]
**Measurement window:** [Date range — typically 14 days post-event]
**Event format:** [In-person / hybrid / dinner / activation]
**Total attendees:** [N]

## Verdict

[One of three: **Measurable lift** / **No detectable lift** / **Insufficient data to conclude.** Then one sentence explaining. E.g. "The San Francisco metro showed +34% CTR and +28% conversion rate vs. the rest-of-country baseline during the 14 days following the event — meaningful lift, though partially confounded by simultaneous paid promotion in the region (see caveats)."]

## The numbers

| Metric | [Target region] | Rest of country | Lift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impressions | [N] | [N] | n/a (different scale) |
| CTR | [X]% | [Y]% | [+Z]% |
| CPC | $[X] | $[Y] | [±Z]% |
| Conversion rate | [X]% | [Y]% | [+Z]% |
| Cost per conversion | $[X] | $[Y] | [±Z]% |

## What we think happened

[2-3 sentences naming the most likely causal story. Be specific. "Awareness of [company] in the region appears to have lifted post-event — CTR climbed for both branded and non-branded campaigns, suggesting the lift is broader than just people who attended the event re-engaging. Conversion rate also climbed, which is consistent with regional awareness translating into intent."]

## What's confounded

This analysis can't cleanly separate:
- **Event-driven lift vs. paid-promotion lift.** We ran $[X] of geo-targeted paid promotion in the region during this window. Some of the measured lift is attributable to that spend, not the event itself.
- **Event week vs. seasonal patterns.** [If the event was around a known regional event/holiday/industry conference, name it.]
- **The event's reach beyond attendees.** [N] people attended; the regional digital lift involves [N times that] people. We can't directly verify how many non-attendees were aware of the event through word-of-mouth or social.

## What we'd do differently next time to measure better

- Run a "geo holdout" — a matched comparison region with similar profile but no event and no paid promotion. Makes lift attribution cleaner.
- Capture attendee-specific identifiers (email, LinkedIn URL) at registration so we can track those individuals' downstream activity vs. non-attendees.
- Measure for 30 days post-event, not 14. B2B sales cycles are long enough that 14-day windows under-attribute pipeline impact.

## What this tells us about future events in [region]

[The decision-oriented section. 2-3 sentences. "The regional lift suggests the event format and audience fit are working in [region]. Recommend repeating the format here next quarter and testing the same format in [comparable region] to see if it generalizes." Or: "Without clearer signal it's hard to recommend a repeat. Suggest the next event in [region] include a geo holdout so we can measure more cleanly."]

## Cost framing (optional, for leadership audiences)

[Include only if a leader is in the audience. Total event cost: $[X]. If we attribute [conservative %] of the regional lift to the event, the cost per incremental conversion is $[Y] — comparing to our blended CPA of $[Z] across paid channels. Or: "Pipeline contribution from event attendees: $[X] in qualified opportunities created in the 30 days following — too early to call ROI but the early signal is [positive / neutral / weak]."]

---

## Tone notes

- The verdict at the top is the most-read sentence. Get it right. Don't overstate — claiming "measurable lift" when the data is noisy will erode trust over the next few events.
- The "what's confounded" section is the credibility move. Field marketers who surface what they can't measure get taken more seriously than ones who claim certainty.
- The "what we'd do differently" section gives leadership a path forward for better measurement. It's also a defensive move — naming the gaps in the measurement methodology preempts criticism.
- "What this tells us about future events" is the section the field marketing manager will quote in their next planning meeting. Make it actionable.
- The cost framing section is optional. Include it when the audience includes someone who controls field marketing budget. Skip it for an internal post-mortem with peers.
