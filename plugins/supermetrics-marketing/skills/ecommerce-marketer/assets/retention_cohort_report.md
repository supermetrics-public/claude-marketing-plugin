# Retention cohort report template

The doc summarizing cohort retention and unit economics for a subscription or repeat-purchase business. Audience: marketing leader + CFO; sometimes the board.

The shape:
- Headline (retention pattern direction)
- Retention curves overview
- Cohort-level economics table
- Payback and LTV
- Trends and risks

## What this implies for marketing

- **Acquisition channels**: if recent cohort LTV is holding but CAC is rising, the gap is at the top of funnel. Either ad costs are inflating or acquisition quality is shifting. Channel-level CAC breakdown (see customer_acquisition_brief) is the next step.
- **Retention investment**: if M3 retention is declining, marketing investment should shift toward post-purchase nurture, lifecycle email, or product-improvement signaling. Retention saves don't get credited to a channel but are the highest-leverage growth lever.
- **Promo strategy**: heavy-discount cohorts typically retain worse. If recent cohorts trace back to a deep-discount period, surface that.

## Caveats

- **Recent-cohort projection risk**: M12 LTV for cohorts younger than 12 months is extrapolated, not measured. The doc shows actuals where data exists and extrapolations elsewhere — labeled accordingly.
- **Attribution**: CAC numerator includes [the user's chosen scope — paid marketing only, all marketing, fully-loaded with team costs]. Whichever choice, be explicit.
- **Churn definition**: in subscription contexts, "active" means [the user's specific definition — last paid in this period, has unpaused subscription, etc.].

## Tone notes

- The retention chart is the most-quoted output. Make sure it's clean — labels, distinct line colors for each cohort, marked horizon lines at M3/M6/M12.
- Don't claim LTV improvement from short-tenure cohorts. They literally haven't had time.
- For SaaS audiences, monthly retention curves are the standard. For ecommerce, repeat-purchase rate curves can substitute.
- Length: 3-4 pages depending on cohort count.
