# Pre-promo planning doc template

The doc presenting the upcoming promotional period's budget plan with targets, assumptions, and pacing. Audience: marketing leader, finance, media buying team.

The shape:
- Headline (the ask + projected outcome)
- Targets per metric
- Per-channel allocation
- Daily pacing
- Anomaly thresholds
- Post-promo measurement plan

## Anomaly thresholds (when to alert)

During the promo, the team should alert if any of these trigger:
- **Daily cost per purchase**: > [N]x baseline ($[X]) for any channel
- **Hourly ROAS**: < [N] sustained for >2 hours on any channel with active spend
- **Zero-conversion ad sets**: any ad set with $[X]+ spend and zero conversions in a 4-hour window
- **Total spend pacing**: actual daily spend more than [%] above or below the plan

(These get fed into the daily anomaly check workflow.)

## Assumptions

- AOV grows [%] year-over-year based on [reason — price changes, mix shift]
- Channel-level CPA holds at last year's levels (no major auction-cost inflation)
- The promo mechanic ([%] off / BOGO / etc.) matches last year's depth
- Inventory levels support projected volume on all SKUs being promoted

## What's at risk

- **CPM inflation during promo windows** — every brand bids harder. CPCs could rise [%]+, increasing cost per purchase even at stable conversion rates.
- **Tracking under load** — pixels and conversion tracking degrade as traffic spikes. Expect 5-15% measurement drop.
- **Inventory stockouts** on the [N] highest-velocity SKUs. Pre-promo inventory check needed by [date].

## Post-promo measurement plan

Within 5 business days of promo end:
- Compare actuals to plan per metric
- Per-channel post-mortem: which channels exceeded vs missed projections
- New customer acquisition count (vs returning) — was this a net acquisition promo?
- Inventory clearance vs holdback — did the promo move slow-moving inventory or overstock the wrong items?
- Comparison to last year's same-period for true year-over-year picture

## Tone notes

- The conservative/plan/stretch framing replaces single-point forecasts. Finance partners take it more seriously than "we'll do $X."
- "What's at risk" is the credibility section. Plans that don't acknowledge risk get cut by finance.
- The anomaly thresholds should be specific enough that a media buyer monitoring the campaign at 11pm knows what to do.
- Length: 3-5 pages. Promo plans get printed and circulated.
