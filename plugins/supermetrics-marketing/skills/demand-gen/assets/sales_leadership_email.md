# Sales leadership email template

The email to the VP of Sales summarizing a budget reallocation based on traffic-to-conversion analysis. The audience is different from a CMO — sales leaders care about lead quality and pipeline impact, not marketing efficiency for its own sake. Translate the analysis up the funnel.

---

**Subject:** Marketing budget shift to improve [lead quality / pipeline efficiency] for [period]

Hi [VP Sales name],

Wanted to flag a budget shift we're making in [time period], because it should show up on your side as [more / fewer / different] leads from [channel].

**What we found:** Over the last [time window], [source channel] generated [X] clicks at [reasonable CPC], but only [Y]% of that traffic converted on-site per GA4. [Destination channel] generated fewer raw clicks but the GA4-measured conversion rate was [Z]x higher — translating to a true CPA of $[X] vs. $[Y].

**What we're doing:** Shifting $[amount] from [source channel] to [destination channel] for [period].

**What this should mean for the sales team:**
- Expecting roughly the same volume of [leads / form fills] going to sales, possibly slightly lower, but **lead quality should improve** — the destination channel produces traffic that engages further on-site before converting.
- We'll know within 2-3 weeks whether the assumption holds. I'll send a follow-up after [date] with what we're seeing.
- If MQL volume drops without a corresponding quality lift, we'll reverse the shift.

**What I need from you:** Just a heads-up — no approval required. If your team flags a change in lead quality (positive or negative) in the next few weeks, please loop me in directly so we can attribute it correctly.

Full data and the channel-by-channel breakdown attached. Happy to walk through it whenever works.

[Sender name]

---

## Tone notes

- Lead with what changes for sales, not what changes for marketing. Sales leaders read marketing emails looking for "what does this mean for my number?" — answer that in the first paragraph.
- Don't make this an approval request unless you actually need approval. Sales leaders get a lot of approval-requesting emails and appreciate a "here's what we're doing, FYI" message that's clearly marked as such.
- Name the assumption that could be wrong, and the date you'll know. This is the difference between "marketing is doing stuff" and "marketing is running a controlled change." Sales leaders trust the second one a lot more.
- Don't send the deck. Sales leaders won't open it. The email itself is the deliverable.
- If sending via Gmail draft connector, save as draft — the user will want to add personal context (an inside joke, a reference to a recent conversation) before sending.
