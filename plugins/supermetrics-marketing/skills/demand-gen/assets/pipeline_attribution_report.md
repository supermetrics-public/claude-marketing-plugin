# Pipeline attribution report template

The doc translating marketing channel spend into pipeline contribution for a sales-leadership audience. Audience: VP of Sales, CRO, or sales-marketing alignment meetings.

The shape:
- Headline (the one number sales cares about)
- Per-channel attribution table
- Caveats (attribution model, time window, pipeline ≠ revenue)
- Recommendation

## Caveats

This view has known limitations. Read with these in mind:

- **Attribution method.** Numbers shown use [first-touch / last-touch / multi-touch] attribution. Using a different model would shift the channel rankings. First-touch credits top-of-funnel channels; last-touch credits closing channels.
- **Pipeline ≠ revenue.** This is opportunities created, not deals closed. Historical close rate from pipeline in this stage is approximately [%]. Applying that, expected revenue is approximately $[expected].
- **Time window.** Opportunities can sit in pipeline for [typical sales cycle]. The 90-day window captures opps created; revenue from those opps will land in [later period].
- **Self-attributed channels.** Some channels (e.g. direct, referral) are likely under-credited because last-touch attribution treats them as the source even when paid channels seeded the contact earlier.

### Follow-up analysis to consider

If sales wants to dig deeper, useful follow-ups:
- **Win rate by channel** — of opps from each channel, what % closed? Lets us compare quality, not just volume.
- **Deal size by channel** — average opp amount per channel. Sometimes the highest-CPO channel generates the largest deals.
- **Time-to-close by channel** — which channels generate fast-closing pipeline vs slow-developing pipeline.

---

## Tone notes

- The headline must include the dollar number. Sales leaders measure in dollars, not in ratios.
- The caveats section is non-optional. Sales has heard "marketing attribution" before; surface the limits before they do.
- Recommendations need to be specific channels and dollar amounts, not "continue investing in winners."
- Length: 1-2 pages. Sales leaders will skim regardless of length.
