# Content promotion brief template

The recommendation document for which content assets should receive the majority of paid promotion budget. This brief is what gets shared with marketing leadership at quarterly planning, so the audience cares about the *why* as much as the *what*.

---

## Paid content promotion: recommendation for [period]
**Period analyzed:** [Date range]
**Platforms reviewed:** [LinkedIn Ads, Facebook Ads, etc.]
**Assets evaluated:** [N]

### Recommendation

Allocate [X]% of the [period] paid promotion budget to these two assets:

1. **[Asset 1 name]** — [budget recommendation, e.g. "~$25k of the $50k total"]
2. **[Asset 2 name]** — [budget recommendation]

Reduce or pause paid promotion for: [list of low-performers, if any]

### Why these two assets

**[Asset 1 name]** ([Asset type — ebook / whitepaper / guide / report])
- CPC: $[X] | CTR: [Y]% | [Lead rate: [Z]%]
- **Why it's resonating:** [The qualitative read. Specific. "The cover frames a problem the audience googles ('how to forecast SaaS pipeline accurately') and the headline names the role explicitly ('for finance teams'). The CTR is highest among the controller/CFO audience, suggesting role-targeted distribution is the engine."]
- **Where it can scale:** [Audience or platform expansion the user should consider]

**[Asset 2 name]** ([Asset type])
- CPC: $[X] | CTR: [Y]% | [Lead rate: [Z]%]
- **Why it's resonating:** [Same treatment]
- **Where it can scale:** [Same treatment]

### What's not working

| Asset | CPC | CTR | Lead rate | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Name] | $[X] | [Y]% | [Z]% | Pause |
| [Name] | $[X] | [Y]% | [Z]% | Reduce by 50% |

[Brief paragraph on the pattern across the under-performers — e.g. "the three under-performing assets all use industry-jargon-heavy titles and generic cover designs. The two winners use plain-language titles and feature a specific role in the artwork. Pattern is consistent enough to inform the next round of asset production."]

### How this changes next-quarter content production

The qualitative read above suggests:
- **Title patterns that work:** [specific, e.g. "role-named ('for finance teams', 'for product marketers'), problem-stated, numbered or quantified"]
- **Title patterns to avoid:** [specific]
- **Cover design themes that work:** [specific]
- **What we still don't know:** [explicit. e.g. "We don't have enough video content to compare formats yet — recommend producing 2 video assets next quarter to establish a baseline."]

### Checkpoint

Review promotion performance again on [date — typically 6 weeks post-shift]. If the top two assets show CPC erosion of more than 25% as spend scales, that's a sign of audience saturation — at that point we either rotate to the next-best assets or refresh the creative.

## Tone notes

- The "why it's resonating" sections are the most-read part of this brief. Marketing leaders are looking for transferable insights for the next round of content, not just a verdict on the current round. Spend the most time there.
- "Resonating" needs to mean something specific. Don't write "speaks to the audience" — write what about the asset speaks to which audience and how you know.
- The "what we still don't know" section is the mature move. Teams that surface their gaps look more credible than teams that pretend they have everything figured out.
- Avoid recommending more than 2 assets get the majority of budget. Concentration is the point — paid promotion works best when you back winners hard, not when you spread budget evenly across "pretty good" assets.
- The recommendation can be re-stated as a one-liner at the top, which is what'll get quoted in Slack or in a planning meeting. Make sure that one line stands alone.
