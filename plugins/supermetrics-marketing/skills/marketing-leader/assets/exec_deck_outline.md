# Executive deck outline template

The structure for a 90-day cross-channel marketing performance deck. The audience is the CMO or leadership team — they have 10-15 minutes for this deck and a lot of other things on their mind. Build for skim-ability.

The `scripts/exec_summary_builder.py` script will generate a populated version of this outline from the raw data. Use this template as a reference for what each slide is supposed to contain and why.

---

## Slide 1 — Headline summary
**Purpose:** Tell the whole story in one slide. If the meeting got cut to 2 minutes, this is the one slide that would play.

**What to include:**
- Big metric box: total spend, total revenue, blended ROAS, total conversions, blended CPA
- One-sentence callout that frames the quarter: "Spend held steady, revenue up 12%, blended ROAS climbed from 2.4x to 2.7x — efficiency gains came primarily from [channel/initiative]."

**What to avoid:** Multiple narratives. Pick one. The detail slides will substantiate it.

---

## Slides 2 to N — Channel deep dives (one per channel)
**Purpose:** Each channel gets one slide so leaders can see where each major investment landed.

**What to include per slide:**
- Channel name as the title
- Spend, revenue, conversions, ROAS, CPA
- Share of total spend and share of total revenue (this is the key framing — is this channel pulling its weight?)
- One callout: what's the channel-specific story? Tested a new audience? Scaled what was working? Refreshed creative mid-quarter?

**What to avoid:** Cramming sub-campaign detail. If a particular campaign deserves attention, give it its own slide rather than burying it on the channel slide.

---

## Slide N+1 — Cross-channel comparison
**Purpose:** Side-by-side comparison so leaders can spot the efficiency winner and the volume driver.

**What to include:**
- Table: channel, spend, revenue, ROAS, CPA
- Callout that explicitly names the efficiency winner (highest ROAS) and the volume driver (most conversions) — these are often different channels, and naming both prevents the "just pour everything into ROAS winner" mistake.

---

## Slide N+2 — Quarter-over-quarter (if comparison data is available)
**Purpose:** Trajectory matters more than absolute numbers. A 2.5x ROAS quarter is great if last quarter was 1.8x, and concerning if last quarter was 3.2x.

**What to include:**
- Headline metrics with % change vs. prior period
- Callout that names what changed: efficiency gained, scale gained, both, or efficiency held while scale grew (or the inverse).

---

## Slide N+3 — Strategic recommendations
**Purpose:** This is the slide leaders actually engage with. Everything before it is context for this.

**What to include:**
- 3 to 5 recommendations max
- Each recommendation must include: what to do, which channel, dollar amount or percent shift, expected impact (with the caveat about diminishing returns where relevant)
- Each recommendation must have an owner

**What to avoid:** Vague language ("optimize", "monitor", "consider"). If you can't write a recommendation as a specific action with a number, it's not ready for this slide.

---

## Slide N+4 — Next-period forecast
**Purpose:** "If we execute the above, here's what next quarter looks like."

**What to include:**
- Projected spend, revenue, conversions, blended ROAS for next period
- Assumption stack: "This forecast assumes (1) Google Ads holds CPA, (2) the proposed LinkedIn budget increase doesn't compress ROAS by more than 10%, (3) creative refresh on Facebook restores Q-1 CTR."

**Why the assumptions matter:** Leaders will challenge the forecast. Surfacing the assumptions upfront turns "your forecast is wrong" into "let's pressure-test assumption #2," which is a more productive conversation.

---

## Slide N+5 — Next steps
**Purpose:** Close the loop. Who does what by when.

**What to include:**
- 3 to 5 concrete actions
- Each line: action — owner — deadline
- Include the next checkpoint date (e.g. "Pacing review on [date]")

**What to avoid:** "Continue monitoring." If it's not an action with an owner and a date, it doesn't belong here.

---

## Overall tone notes

- One chart per slide, max. Two if you must.
- Every slide should answer one question. If the slide answers two, split it.
- Numbers in tables get formatted consistently. $1,200 not $1200; 2.4x not 2.40; 12% not 0.12.
- The callouts are the most-read text on the deck. Spend the most editing time there.
