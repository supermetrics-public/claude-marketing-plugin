# Budget reallocation memo template

The short memo justifying a budget shift between platforms based on ROAS or CPA data. This is what gets attached to a planning doc, sent to finance, or pasted into a Slack thread when someone asks "why are we doing this?"

The constraint that shapes this memo: someone in finance will read it looking for the gap in the logic. Pre-empt that.

---

## [Date] — Budget reallocation: $[amount] from [source channel] to [destination channel]

### Recommendation
Shift $[dollar amount] ([X]% of [source channel]'s current monthly budget) from [source channel] to [destination channel] for [period].

### Why

Over the [time window] period, the two channels performed as follows:

| Channel | Spend | Revenue | ROAS | CPA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Destination channel] | $[X] | $[Y] | [Z]x | $[W] |
| [Source channel] | $[X] | $[Y] | [Z]x | $[W] |

[Destination channel] is generating [N]x the ROAS of [source channel] on equivalent spend. At current efficiency, the recommended shift would produce an additional ~$[expected revenue] in [period] without increasing total spend.

### What this assumes
- **[Destination channel] has headroom.** This recommendation assumes [destination] can absorb the additional spend without compressing efficiency more than 10%. We'll know within 2 weeks of the shift — if marginal ROAS drops below [target threshold], we reassess.
- **Attribution model held constant.** Both channels' ROAS figures use [the same attribution model — name it]. Switching attribution would change the comparison and invalidate this recommendation.
- **No seasonal effect.** The [time window] period was free of [Black Friday / quarter close / specific event] effects. If next period contains those, the shift may need adjusting.

### What we're not doing
- Not zeroing out [source channel]. It still generates measurable [conversions / brand value / specific contribution] at [its current ROAS]. The shift is partial, not full.
- Not increasing total budget. This is a reallocation, not an ask for more dollars.

### Checkpoint
Review pacing and ROAS on [destination channel] on [date — typically 2 weeks post-shift]. If marginal ROAS holds, consider a further shift the following month.

---

## Tone notes

- Lead with the recommendation, then justify. Finance partners value memos that don't make them dig.
- The "what this assumes" section is the most valuable part of the memo. Marketing teams that surface assumptions get more autonomy than teams that don't.
- The "what we're not doing" section heads off the "wait, are you killing [source channel]?" reaction. It's worth a paragraph.
- The checkpoint date converts the recommendation from a one-way door into a two-week experiment, which is much easier to approve.
- Length: this memo should fit on one page. If it doesn't, you've over-justified.
