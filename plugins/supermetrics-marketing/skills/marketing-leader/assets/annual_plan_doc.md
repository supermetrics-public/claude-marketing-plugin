# Annual marketing plan template

The structured doc for the annual marketing plan output. Audience: CFO, CEO, board. The plan needs to defend a budget request with evidence, not vibes.

The shape:
- Executive summary on page 1 (decision-ready)
- Current state (what's been happening)
- Channel portfolio analysis (the quadrant view)
- Scenarios with trade-offs
- Recommendation with rationale
- Risks and assumptions

---

**Trade-off:** No upside. Doesn't capture growing-channel opportunity.

### Scenario B — Growth (+[%] total, $[X])

**Trade-off:** Lower total reach. Best for capital efficiency / runway extension.

## Checkpoint and reforecast

We propose reforecasting at the [quarter / half-year] mark, specifically reviewing:
- Are the growing channels still growing at the assumed rate?
- Has the declining channel sunset gone as planned?
- Has CAC held at the blended target?

If any of these diverge from the plan by more than 15%, we'd propose a mid-period reallocation.

## Tone notes

- The Executive Summary needs to be readable in 60 seconds. If your CFO has 60 seconds and reads only that, they should know the ask, the math, and the strategic shift.
- "What didn't work" is the highest-credibility section. Skip it and you read as overselling.
- Scenarios should be genuinely different. Don't present "5% more, 10% more, 15% more" — that's a continuum, not scenarios. Each scenario should embody a different strategy.
- The risks table is non-optional. CFOs will ask "what if..." regardless; surfacing them yourself shows you've thought about them.
- Length: 4-8 pages depending on detail required. If finance wants more detail, they'll ask. Annual plan docs that exceed 12 pages get skimmed.
