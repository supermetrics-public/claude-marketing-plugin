# New channel business case template

The case for testing a new advertising channel. Audience: marketing leader, CFO, or whoever has to approve a test budget. The doc has to balance ambition (here's an opportunity) with discipline (here's how we'll know if it's working and when to pull out).

The shape:
- Why now (saturation evidence + opportunity)
- The test (budget, timeline, success criteria)
- The exit criteria
- Opportunity cost

---

## The test

### Budget and timeline
**Test budget:** $[X] over [N] weeks
**Source:** [Reallocated from {saturated channel}, or new dollars approved by finance]

### What we'll measure
| Metric | Target for "continue" | Target for "exit" |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per [conversion type] | $[X] or better | >$[Y] |
| Conversion volume | At least [N] | Fewer than [M] |
| Quality signal (e.g. MQL rate, AOV) | Within [%] of blended | More than [%] worse |
| Audience overlap with current channels | Lower than [%] | Above [%] (suggests channel just reaches our existing audience) |

These thresholds aren't arbitrary — they're set so a "continue" verdict means the new channel is at least as efficient as the channels we'd reallocate from.

### Phasing
- **Weeks 1-2:** Setup, conservative spend ($[X]/week), learn the platform's targeting and creative requirements
- **Weeks 3-6:** Full test budget pace ($[X]/week), iterate on creative based on early signal
- **Weeks 7-8:** Decision week — evaluate against criteria above, decide continue/scale/exit

### Creative and operational requirements
[What does this channel need that we don't currently produce? E.g. "Short-form video assets, 9:16 aspect ratio, 6-15 seconds. Estimated production cost: $[X]. Existing static assets won't translate."]

## Exit criteria

We'll pull out of the test if **any** of these are true at week 8:
- Cost per [conversion] exceeds [exit threshold]
- Volume is below [exit threshold] despite full spend pace
- Quality signal is materially worse than current channels
- Operational complexity outweighs the marginal benefit (e.g. creative production cost is structurally too high)

A test failing isn't a problem — it's the test working. We get a real answer on whether this channel deserves ongoing investment.

## Opportunity cost

This $[X] test budget comes from [source]. The specific opportunity cost:
- Reducing [current channel] spend by $[X] for [N] weeks → projected loss of [N] conversions at [current CPA]
- vs. potential gain from [new channel] if it hits "continue" thresholds: [N+M] conversions at [target CPA]

Expected value calculation (assuming a [%] probability of the test succeeding): [E[V] = ...]

This is a bet with positive expected value if we believe the test hit rate is above [break-even probability].

## Checkpoint

Mid-test (week 4): if early signal looks materially worse than the "continue" thresholds, propose early termination rather than running the full 8 weeks. Don't waste budget on a test that's clearly failing.

End-of-test (week 8): full evaluation against criteria, decision on continue/scale/exit. If continue, the next step is a follow-up case proposing scale and ongoing budget.

---

## Tone notes

- "Why now" must come first. The case stands or falls on whether the saturation argument is convincing.
- Exit criteria are non-optional. Finance approves tests with clear exit criteria; they reject tests where the loss case is undefined.
- The opportunity cost section is the credibility-builder. Saying "we'll move $50k from Facebook" is different from acknowledging "that $50k could otherwise produce [N] conversions, and we're betting [new channel] will outperform."
- Probability-weighted expected value framing helps with finance audiences who think in those terms.
- Length: 2-4 pages. Anything more starts to feel like overselling.
