# CMO email template

The email summarizing the deck for the CMO. The constraint that shapes everything: the CMO will read this on their phone, in 30 seconds, between meetings. Build for that.

The shape of a good CMO summary email:
- Subject that signals what this is and whether action is needed
- Headline at the top (the one-sentence version of the deck)
- Three bullets of supporting evidence
- A clear ask (or "no ask, FYI" if there isn't one)
- Link or attachment to the full deck

---

**Subject:** [Period] performance summary — [headline like "ROAS up 12%, recommend $40k LinkedIn shift"]

Hi [CMO name],

Quick summary of the [period] performance ahead of [meeting / next planning cycle]:

**The headline:** [One sentence on the overall direction. E.g. "Spend held flat at $[X], revenue grew [Y]% to $[Z], blended ROAS climbed from [A]x to [B]x — efficiency gains came primarily from LinkedIn and a Facebook creative refresh in May."]

**What's working:**
- [Channel / initiative] — [specific metric and context]
- [Channel / initiative] — [specific metric and context]
- [Channel / initiative] — [specific metric and context]

**The ask:**
[One specific thing you need from them. Approve a budget shift. Attend the planning meeting on [date]. Sign off on the recommendation in slide [N]. If there's no ask, write "No ask — sharing for awareness ahead of [meeting]." Don't fake an ask if there isn't one.]

Full deck attached / linked at [URL]. Happy to walk through it whenever works.

[Sender name]

---

## Tone notes

- Subject line should let them decide whether to read it without opening it. "[Period] performance summary" alone is too generic — append the headline finding or the ask.
- "The headline" sentence is the most-edited sentence in this email. If it's not a single sentence that summarizes the whole quarter, rewrite it. Cut everything that isn't load-bearing.
- Three bullets is the cap. Four feels like you're hedging. The bullets should each contain a number — that's what makes them feel like substance and not opinion.
- "The ask" is where most CMO emails go wrong. Marketing teams under-ask because they don't want to seem demanding. CMOs prefer being asked for specific things — it's faster than guessing what you need from them.
- If you're sending this through a Gmail draft (via the connector), put it in their drafts rather than sending directly. They'll want to make tweaks.
